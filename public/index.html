Greater London
Seven weighted dimensions. Every input is published open data. Weights and limitsHide Composite score 0–100 Weights shown to scale
  • 30% Safety police.uk street crimes per 1,000 residents (ONS mid-2024 ward population estimates), single month (June 2026 in this build). The score is the share of London wards with more crime per resident, so 70 means fewer crimes per resident than 70% of wards.
  • 20% Education Ofsted ratings of state-funded schools in the ward, averaged: all Outstanding scores 100, all Good 67, lower ratings pull the average down. Schools without a rating are left out. Snapshot of 31 August 2025. Ofsted stopped issuing single-word grades in September 2024 and moved to report cards in November 2025, so this is a closing stock of older judgements: schools still show them, but each new inspection retires one.
  • 15% Transport Stations in the ward, half-credit for stations one ward over (they serve catchments larger than one ward), and bus stops at a twentieth of a station's weight. Scored as the ward's standing among all London wards. No longer divided by ward area, which unfairly punished wards that are large because of their parks.
  • 12% Green space OpenStreetMap parks, playgrounds and nature reserves inside the ward boundary. Playgrounds also count in the play dimension, on purpose. Here they count as somewhere nearby; in the play dimension they count as space per child.
  • 10% Families nearby Share of households with dependent children (Census 2021), scored as the ward's standing among all London wards: a measure of the families already living there. The share of residents aged 25–49 is shown on ward panels as context but no longer scored, because it tracked young adults in general rather than families.
  • 8% Play provision OS Open Greenspace play and informal recreation space (equipped play areas, playing fields and public parks; formal facilities such as bowling greens and tennis courts excluded) per child aged 0–15, scored as the ward's standing among all London wards. Site areas are clipped to the ward boundary, so a park crossing several wards is split between them. The London Plan Policy S4 figure of 10 m² per child stays on every ward panel and drives the play map layer as a benchmark, but does not cap this score: 84% of wards clear the benchmark once parks count, so capping handed most of London an identical 100.
  • 5% Planning Approved planning applications for child-relevant facilities since 2023 (Planning London Datahub): new or expanded schools, nurseries, playgrounds, health and community centres, youth and children's centres, and new parks and open space. Located to each ward by the application's coordinates. Applications are sorted by keywords in their descriptions, which misfiles some of them.

What the score does not tell you

Why some wards score 0 on safety
Nine wards score 0, for two different reasons. Four have the most recorded crime per resident in London: West End (175 crimes per 1,000 residents), Tower, St James’s and Farringdon Without. All four are places people travel to, so much of the crime is against visitors while the denominator counts only residents. The other five are City of London wards with no published population at all, Bread Street, Bridge, Cordwainer, Cornhill and Langbourn, which sit on an older area-based score and are not comparable with the other 689. Their panels say so. Crime is a single month of police.uk street crime, June 2026 in this build; one month carries noise, and police.uk publishes about two months behind.
Green space and play are measured inside the ward only
Only what lies inside the ward counts. A park crossing a boundary is split between the wards it covers, so each gets its share, but a park wholly over the line counts for nothing however close it is. A future version will measure walking-distance access instead.
15 City of London wards are unscored for play
No published child population, so no denominator. Shown as no data rather than as a low score.
Play counts whole sites, so it is an upper bound
A park counts in full even though not all of it is playable. The equipped-play-only lower bound is shown on every ward panel and in the repository.
Play areas were re-measured in August 2026
Sites used to be assigned whole to the ward holding their centre point. That credited East Sheen with 9.8 km² of play space inside a 6.0 km² ward, because Richmond Park’s centre falls there, and gave neighbouring wards none of the same park. Areas are now clipped to ward boundaries, and the build fails if any ward holds more play space than it has land.
Missing dimensions are excluded, never zeroed
Where a ward lacks an input, the remaining weights are renormalised, so absence is not punished as a bad result.

Every source is open and independently checkable, so if you disagree with an input you can go and check it. KidStreet is not funded by, or affiliated with, any London council.
